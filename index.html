Where design meets operation

Engineering that holds up after start-up, not just on the drawing.

I take a process from flow sheet to instrumented plant, then live with it long enough to learn what the drawings got wrong.

For over two years I’ve been the process engineer on a continuously operating plant, responsible for steam generation, heat exchange, vacuum and gas handling, gas–liquid separation, compressed air, cooling water, water treatment and gas liquefaction. Mass and energy balances, equipment sizing, commissioning and process safety are the core of the job, and they carry across every industry.

I’m looking for my next step in process engineering, operations or research, including doctoral study.

Services

Process engineering for plants, start-ups and project developers, from a single calculation to a full design package.

  • Process design

    Turning a process idea or an existing plant into clear, buildable design documents.

    • Process and block flow diagrams
    • Mass and energy balances
    • Design basis and process descriptions
    • Debottlenecking and design reviews

  • Equipment sizing and specification

    Sizing equipment to the duty it actually sees, then writing the datasheets suppliers quote against.

    • Pumps, blowers, compressors and vacuum systems
    • Heat exchangers, separators and vessels
    • Datasheets and vendor bid comparison
    • Line sizing

  • Water and wastewater treatment

    Matching treatment to the source water and to what the process needs downstream.

    • Water needs assessments and water balances
    • Treatment train selection, softening and RO
    • Wastewater characterisation and options
    • Distribution network hydraulics

  • Utilities engineering

    Keeping the supporting systems reliable, efficient and right-sized.

    • Cooling towers and cooling water
    • Boilers, steam and condensate recovery
    • Compressed air networks
    • Energy and water efficiency assessments

  • Hydraulic and pressure drop studies

    Finding out why a system underperforms and what it takes to fix it.

    • System-wide pressure drop analysis
    • Pump and system curve checks
    • Diagnosing flow starvation and undersizing

  • Instrumentation, control and P&IDs

    Making sure a plant can be measured, controlled and operated safely.

    • P&ID development and ISA-5.1 review
    • Instrument selection and tagging
    • Control philosophy and interlocks

  • Commissioning and start-up

    Bringing new units online in stages, with safety checks at each step.

    • Staged commissioning plans
    • Pre-startup safety reviews
    • Start-up troubleshooting and SOPs

  • Reliability, safety and reporting

    Making performance measurable and failures less likely to repeat.

    • Root cause analysis of recurring faults
    • HAZID/HAZOP participation and chemical safety
    • Measurement uncertainty and KPI reporting
    • Monitoring, evaluation and learning frameworks
